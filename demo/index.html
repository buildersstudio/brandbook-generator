Logo

The Builders wordmark opens with the four-pointed spark — the mark of the studio. Black on cream, white on deep ink. Nothing in between.

Primaryon cream
Inverseon deep ink
Sparkfavicons · avatars · bullets

Clear space

Preserve clear space equal to the height of the spark on all four sides of the wordmark. On photography, always place the white wordmark over the darkest region of the frame or over a gradient scrim.

Rules

Do
  • Use the black wordmark on cream and light surfaces.
  • Use the white wordmark on deep ink, photography, and gradients.
  • Use the spark alone in small spaces — favicons, avatars, list bullets.
  • Keep the wordmark at 100% opacity, always.
Don't
  • Do not stretch, rotate, or distort the wordmark.
  • Do not recolor it into the accent palette.
  • Do not add shadows, outlines, or glows.
  • Do not set it over busy photography without a scrim.

Photography

Real studio photography only — events, founders, Rotterdam, the team at work. When there is no photo, the signature brand gradients take its place: solid for flat grounds, alpha over imagery.

Demo night
Founder weekend
Fireside

Style direction

SubjectReal people at real moments — founders presenting, rooms mid-conversation, the city the studio works from.
LightAmbient and honest. Event light as it was; no studio strobes, no staging.
TreatmentA gradient scrim (ink 0.5 → 0.85, vertical) whenever type sits on the image.
GradientsThe brand gradient family replaces photography on announcement cards — solid variants for flat backgrounds, alpha variants layered over photos.

Rules

Do
  • Use photography from the studio's own library.
  • Keep at least a third of the frame quiet for type.
  • Scrim before setting white copy on an image.
Don't
  • No stock photography, ever.
  • No heavy color grading over the accents.
  • No faces cropped mid-expression for layout convenience.

Messaging

How Builders sounds — on the site, in posts, at events. This chapter is what downstream tools read first.

Voice

Direct, confident, builder-to-builder.
Short declaratives. Concrete numbers and dates. The studio talks like a founder who has shipped, not like a fund that observes. One Instrument Serif italic flourish per asset — the emphasis, never the whole line.
Avoid
Hype adjectivesVague timelinesFund-speak
Prefer
Concrete datesNamed eventsPlain verbs

Headline formula

[KICKER IN DM MONO] + [Declarative in Favorit XB, one serif-italic emphasis]
Where bold founders build stellar companies.
Supporting copy: what happens, when, and for whom — in that order, three sentences at most. CTA: a date beats an adjective (“Applications close August 15” over “Apply now”).

What we talk about

10 weeks
The program — idea to company, in the studio, on a clock.
Staged
Capital released as milestones land, not up front.
VSI
The venture intelligence platform underneath every venture.

Headline examples

Where bold founders build stellar companies.

Demo Night — the batch ships.

Rules

Do
  • Lead with what happens and when.
  • Use real numbers, real dates, real events.
  • Keep one serif-italic flourish per asset, at most.
Don't
  • Banned phrasings: founder counts, “one crew”, “side by side”, “prove it in ten weeks”, “the work is the application”.
  • Don't bake a countdown and post it days later.
  • Don't stack more than one idea per sentence.
